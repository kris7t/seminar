# slider

Scientific talks and lectures in Markdown

## Basics

Markdown in, slides out

### Why slider? {#why}

- Keep your slides in plain text under version control
- Write content in Markdown, style it with themes
- Present from any modern web browser

:::notes
Mention that the deck is a single HTML file after `vite build`.
:::

### Writing slides

- `#` starts the title slide, `##` a section, `###` a content slide
- `depth:` in the frontmatter shifts these levels, and defaults to the deepest
  heading level
- `---` starts a slide without a title, and `::slide[Title]` one with a title
- Slide ids come from the titles, e.g., `#writing-slides`

:step End a heading with `{#id .class section}` to set slide props, where the
bare word is a master of the theme, and `master=` takes an imported one, like
`ClosingMaster`.

## Steps

Revealing content in steps

### Revealing content

- List items are shown up front, or along with the previous step
- :step Add `:step` to reveal an item in a new step
- and the items after it along with it
- :step Nested lists can have steps of their own
  - :step like this one

:step `:step` in a paragraph reveals the paragraph

:::notes
Press **o** for the overview, which keeps the steps shown on each slide.

Notes that don't fit below the slide can be expanded.
:::

### Interleaving steps

- :step{group=pro with=initial} Markdown is easy to write
- :step{group=con} but layout control is limited
- :step{group=pro} Diffs are readable
- :step{group=con} but complex figures still need components

### Step styles {steps=dim}

- :step `{steps=dim}` on the slide dims past steps
- :step so that the audience follows the current one
- :step{preview} `:step{preview}` shows a step ghosted in advance
  - :step But it can still have nested steps
- :step{focus} `:step{focus}` dims it both before and after

:step{only} `:step{only}` shows a callout only during its step

:step And then it's gone

:step Poof

### Callouts

- :step{group=parse} Parsing turns Markdown into a syntax tree
- :step{group=render} Rendering turns the tree into React elements

:step{in=parse only} `:step{in=parse}` nests a callout in the step of
the `parse` group, wherever it is on the slide

:step{in=parse scoped} `{only}` callouts go after their turn, but
`{scoped}` ones like this stay

:step{in=parse scoped} until their item is past

:step{in=render focus} Callouts also look at least as hidden as their item

### Ranges of steps {after=1 before=4}

- :step `{after=1}` skips this step when going through the slide
- :step so the slide starts here
- :step and `{before=4}` ends it after this step
- :step The step tree (Shift+T) can still show this one

### Recaps {to=1}

- :step `{to=1}` stops this slide after its first step
- :step and `::recap[#recaps]` shows it again later, continuing from there

## Typesetting

Formulas with MathJax

### Math

Inline math like $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$ is rendered with MathJax when the deck
is compiled, with MathML for screen readers.

$$
\begin{equation}
  \hat{f}(\xi) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\, e^{-2\pi i x \xi} \,\mathrm{d}x
  \label{fourier}
\end{equation}
$$

- :step Equations are numbered, and $\eqref{fourier}$ refers back to them
- :step The math font comes from the theme

::recap[#recaps]

## Backup {noslide spare}

### Spare slides

- `{spare}` on a slide skips it when going through the deck
- On a section, it applies to the slides in the section, unless they have
  `{spare=false}`
- Choose a spare slide from the contents, the overview, or with G when a
  question comes up, and the arrow keys go on to the spare slides after it

### Going back

Going back from a spare slide shows the one before it, even if it's not spare.

### Divisions without slides

`{noslide}` on the Backup heading groups these slides in the contents and the
overview without a section slide.
